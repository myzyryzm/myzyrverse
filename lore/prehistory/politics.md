# Politics at story start (1160 BCE)

## The Assyrian war plan
- Assyria is expanding in the east and has been raiding cities in northern Canaan.
- Ramesses III wants war for two reasons: to protect northern Canaan (and bind it to Egypt through Tyre), and to expand Egypt's power, reach and his own legacy.
- **Isis** pushes for it because Tyre is her homeland.
- **The nobles** oppose it because of the cost.

## The Babylon–Elam embassy (early in the story)
- Ramesses III hosts the kings of Babylon and Elam, or their envoys, to bring them into an alliance against Assyria.
- Assyria borders Babylon. Joined together, the two could weaken Assyria and divide its land.
- Babylon and Elam have never gotten along. Babylon plays the **big brother** and Elam the **little brother**, and the meeting should feel tense in exactly that way.
- **Later in the story, Elam attacks Babylon.** Historically, Elam sacked Babylon around 1158 BCE and carried off the stele of Hammurabi.

## The nobility
- Rich families. Some claim distant royal blood; most simply grew rich over generations. Their wealth comes from:
  - trade,
  - great farm estates worked by tenant families, or
  - mines, such as Nubian gold.
- They profited from the trade routes, which are now shrinking, and they have paid for Ramesses III's wars.
- They cannot object openly. Not wanting to pay for another war is their main motive for removing him.

## Internal unrest
- The **first recorded labor strike in history** (the tomb-builders of Deir el-Medina) happens early in the story, before the assassination.
- It is started by nefarious people and can be a precursor to the assassination.

## The conspiracy
- Tiye and the nobles both benefit from removing Ramesses III.
- Tiye needs the older princes absent, not killed. No royal children die.
- After the assassination, a **princess of Assyria** arrives. She has been promised to Pentawer, Tiye's son, now pharaoh, and they marry.
  - Egypt and Assyria are now joined by marriage, which leaves Tyre in a precarious position.
  - Fighting follows between Tyre and Assyria.
- In effect, the coup also reverses Egypt's foreign policy, from war with Assyria to alliance with it.
