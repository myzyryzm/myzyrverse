# Realms and places

## Egypt
- **Capital:** Pi-Ramesses, on the Nile in the eastern Delta.
- **Nubia** (the former kingdom of Kush) is part of Egypt, effectively a vassal.
  - It has gold mines, run by a Nubian noble family that may descend from the old Nubian kings.
  - The miners are Nubians: not slaves, but badly paid and overworked.
- **Southern Canaan** (e.g. Gaza) is part of Egypt.
- Its western neighbors are the Libyans, invaders whom Ramesses III has repelled.

## Northern Canaan and Tyre
- Northern Canaan is **not** part of Egypt; in practice it is Tyre.
- Over the last 20–50 years Tyre has become the dominant power there, much more independent of Egypt than before.
- **Tyre** is a merchant hub and still makes very good money in the Mediterranean despite the Collapse.
- The city is laid out as it was before Alexander's causeway: the royals and rich merchant families live on the island, everyone else on the mainland. What the mainland city looks like is undecided.

## Cyprus
- Its role is undecided. It might be the purple guild's base.

## Greece (the Mycenaean world)
- Home of the Sea Peoples: broken palaces of Cyclopean masonry, and raiders.
- A few palaces still stand; most are destroyed.
- Beasts and mythical creatures live there:
  - Cyclopes, being driven out of their caves by the Drought;
  - wolves and werewolves.

## Crete
- The palace of **Knossos**, now run-down. The Minotaur is still there.
- A possible Sherden training ground.

## Anatolia
- The Hittite empire has fallen, and so has its capital, **Hattusa**. The region is run-down.
- Possibly the epicenter of the Drought and its beasts *(open)*.

## Assyria
- A power expanding in the east and raiding northern Canaan.
- Ramesses III's planned enemy. After the coup, it is bound to Egypt by marriage.

## Babylon and Elam
- They have never gotten along. Babylon is the big brother and Elam the little brother.
- Elam attacks Babylon at some point in the story.

## The Steppe *(undecided)*
- The Andronovo culture of Central Asia.
- Possibly Scythians (anachronistic for 1160, but allowed) as the precursors of the Amazons, or as the Amazons themselves: warrior women and men.
