This lore run adds the griffin as a real, natural animal (no magic, no purple). It is wingless and flightless, with a big cat's body, a vulture's beak and an upright crest, and it leaps farther than any lion. Adults reach cave-lion size. Long ago men loosed griffins in battle and harnessed them to chariots, but never rode them. They were the guardian and sacred animals of the Minoan palaces, and the Knossos Throne Room paintings are portraits of them. Men with griffins once fought an ancient war against the Cyclopes, who scattered across northern Greece, Anatolia and the steppe; the enmity survives in griffin instinct and human legend. By 1160 griffins are thought extinct, but a small feral remnant survives on Mount Ida in Crete, and the Drought is driving it down toward people. Griffins naturally sense the Drought's beasts, but they are its casualties, not a cure. During Sherden training on Crete, Jon finds a single orphaned juvenile. He earns its trust by protecting it rather than taming it (he refuses to kill or cage it), and it bonds to him alone as a parent-substitute, slowly, through his voice. It travels with him through Greece and Anatolia, and early on it is a danger and a mouth to feed more than an ally.
