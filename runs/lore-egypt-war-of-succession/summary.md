Lore run covering the War of Succession (1190–1185). It was a north–south split. Seti II, the elder brother and legitimate heir, held Pi-Ramesses and the Delta. Amenmesse, the younger and more warlike brother, was Viceroy of Kush when their father died and crowned himself at Thebes, backed by Nubian gold and the priesthood of Amun. The war was mostly stalemate and betrayal, and the Deir el-Medina tomb-builders' village was fought over and ransacked. After Amenmesse died, his teenage son Siptah was made king by an unnamed foreign-born kingmaker. The kingmaker arranged the Twosret marriage and planned Seti II's killing. Once Siptah held the whole throne, he and Twosret executed the kingmaker, at Twosret's demand. The kingmaker's clients then defected to Setnakhte, who broke Siptah within the year. Setnakhte cast himself as Seti II's avenger and chosen heir and erased Amenmesse and Siptah from the record. In 1160, temple scribes teach an official history with no rival kings in it, and only old men remember the truth, so any surviving child of Siptah would expose the dynasty's founding lie.
