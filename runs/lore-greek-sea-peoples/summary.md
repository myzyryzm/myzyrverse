This lore run sets out who the Greek Sea Peoples are in 1160. They are what is left of the Mycenaean palaces' own war-fleets, cut loose when the Drought broke the ration system that paid rowers and coastguards. The crews kept their ships and went on raiding for themselves, led by former headmen (qa-si-re-u) and ship-captains whose rank is counted in hulls and oars. The few palaces still standing (Mycenae, Tiryns and Athens) are held by "cistern kings" who walled in their water using knowledge taken from the Cyclopes. Mycenae has not burned yet, and the story will decide what causes its first burning. Raiding centres on taking people: coasts are emptying and villagers are moving into fortified peak refuges. Greek bards remember the failed invasions of Egypt, ending with Ramesses III's Year 8 victory (~1177), as a great war followed by bad homecomings, and Ramesses III is the villain of their songs. Troy also exists. The author has settled that the Sherden came from Sardinia and have no connection to the Greek Sea Peoples, though ordinary Egyptians lump both together as foreign invaders. This overrides the prehistory line that Ramesses II recruited "Sea Peoples".
