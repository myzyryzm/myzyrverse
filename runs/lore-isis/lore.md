# Isis, Great Royal Wife

## Summary
- Great Royal Wife of Ramesses III, about 45 in 1160 (born ~1205).
- Princess of Tyre, whose royal house is enormously rich from trade. She knows how rich it is.
- Mother of the heir Aegon, of Helen, and of Jon. A fourth child is possible but undecided.
- She is entitled and holds rank above everything. She looks down on the common-born secondary wife, Tiye.
- She presses for war with Assyria to protect her homeland.
- She survives the harem conspiracy, probably because she is away from Pi-Ramesses when it strikes. Where she is and which child is with her are still open.

## Origins: a young royal house
- Tyre's royalty is new and fragile, roughly two generations old.
- In the Egyptian archives, Tyre's rulers appear as Pharaoh's vassals. They fell at his feet "seven times and seven times" and called him "my sun." They begged him for water and firewood for an island city that could not supply itself from its own ground.
- Old Egyptian families still remember this.
- Within recent memory, Tyre has risen to dominate northern Canaan. It became a house that Pharaoh marries as an equal.
- That rise rests on the murex beds off Tyre's coast. They are Tyre's control of the raw purple supply.
- Isis knows Tyre's vassal past. She does not believe her house has always been royal.
- She holds herself above everyone because she knows how short the distance is. One slip would make her a servant's daughter again in the eyes of the old court.

## Girlhood: the fall of the north
- Isis was a girl of about 13 to 15, living on the island, when Ugarit and the northern ports fell (~1192–1190).
- Tyre served as harbour-master of the coast and took in wrecked ships and their crews.
- Ugarit's king had sent his army north to help the Hittites. The relief came only after his city had been sacked.
- The lesson she took: allies arrive late, and a city on the sea stands alone.
- Assyria's raids on the dye coast now look to her like the start of Ugarit again.

## The exported sister
- When her marriage to the new 20th-Dynasty king was arranged (~1185–1182, when she was about 20 to 23), Isis had a real claim to rule in Tyre.
  - The claim came either from shared rule with her brother or from being their father's favourite. Which one is not settled.
- Her brother, or the merchant houses behind him, settled the rivalry by sending her to Egypt. Nobody had to die.
- The marriage was done to her. It was not a bargain she struck.
- The match served both sides:
  - Egypt wanted it to draw northern Canaan back into its orbit.
  - Tyre wanted it to remove a rival claimant.

## The marriage contract
- **Rank.** Tyre's price copied Hatti's price for Ramesses II's Hittite bride. Isis had to be named Great Royal Wife, not one more foreign wife lodged in the khener.
  - The house that exported her against her will therefore also fought for her rank. It was protecting what it had invested in her.
- **Dowry.** It was paid in Tyre's own wealth:
  - dyed purple wool and finished cloth;
  - silver;
  - cedar;
  - the use of Tyrian ships for Egyptian troops.
- **Journey.** Egyptian soldiers met her caravan and escorted it down the coast road to Pi-Ramesses.
- **Household.** She came with a sworn Tyrian household:
  - ladies;
  - scribes who write Akkadian and Canaanite;
  - her own priests.
- That household is a small Tyre inside the khener, with its own budget and its own envoys.

## Marriage to Ramesses III
- His position:
  - He is the son of Setnakhte, Seti II's top general, who took the throne at the end of the War of Succession.
  - His royal blood is distant.
  - He is obsessed with legacy and wants to equal Ramesses II.
- Isis loved him and still loves him. The love is not lust or romance. She respects him, and she bore his children.
- They are real allies. The planned Assyrian war is the thing they built together. It joins his legacy to her homeland's safety.
- She is not a warhawk for its own sake. She wants her home protected, and the war is the means.
- The war's cost falls on the nobility, who resent it and cannot say so.
- The real fault line in the marriage is Aegon (see below), not Tiye. Ramesses despises his heir's degeneracy, and Isis cannot agree with him.

## Children
- **Aegon**, the eldest prince and heir, is in his late teens to early 20s.
  - He drinks, chases women, and takes black-market purple.
  - She cannot admit he is unfit. A failed heir of her blood would disprove the creed her pride rests on.
  - She shields him and blames others: his father's contempt, his companions, the black-market purple trade.
  - She has a fairly good idea how bad his purple use is, and she defends him anyway. Her defence of him is self-defence dressed as a mother's love.
  - If he grows into a worthy man, it would vindicate her and humble her at once.
- **Helen** is Aegon's younger sister and his wife or bride-to-be. The marriage is loveless. Isis's part in arranging it is not established.
- **Jon** is about five years younger than Aegon. He chose Sherden service and trains abroad on Crete. No particular relationship between Isis and Jon is established yet.
- Some of Isis's entitlement shows in her children.

## Tiye
- Tiye married Ramesses III at about 15 (~1175). Isis was about 30 then and had already borne the heir. (These ages are derived from current numbers, which are still in flux.)
- Isis looks down on Tiye's common birth and expects Tiye, like everyone, to keep her on a pedestal.
- She thinks secondary wives are beneath jealousy. Her contempt is about rank, not the bed.
- Her weapon is form. She will not address an inferior by a rank she has not granted.
- Her scorn for Tiye is the scorn she fears from Egypt's old families, turned onto someone below her.
- In the eyes of the old court, both women are upstarts. Neither acknowledges this.

## Tyre, the purple and the guild
- Tyre's royal house knows the murex beds are its strongest lever over the purple guild.
- Assyria's raids on northern Canaan are partly aimed at Tyre's dye coast. Her homeland's safety and the purple supply are the same fight.
- Isis learns of her husband's death through the guild's relay. It is the same network that carried the conspirators' messages in the same season and warned no one.

## The coup and after (planned)
- The conspiracy kills Ramesses III and puts Pentawer, Tiye's son, on the throne. Isis survives. No royal children die.
- The coup reverses Egypt's foreign policy, from war with Assyria to alliance with it. It is therefore aimed at her policy as much as at her husband.

- Her course runs from grief to revenge. She mourns the man as well as the war they planned.
- **If she is in Tyre when the coup strikes** (the likeliest placement; still open):
  - She arrives not as a guest but as a former rival returning home.
  - She brings an Egyptian queen's title and her sworn household.
  - Tyre at that moment needs someone to lead its war against Assyria.
  - Her household gives her a ready escort and a route.
- Who leads Tyre's war, her brother or a son of hers, is open.

## Divergences from the historical record
- **Isis herself.** The historical Great Royal Wife of Ramesses III, Iset Ta-Hemdjert, was of unknown origin. Her mother's name is sometimes read as Syrian. Here she is a princess of Tyre.
- **Tyre's dominance.** Tyre leading northern Canaan by 1160 is deliberate. Historically, Sidon was usually the senior Phoenician city in this period.
- **Her rank.** No Levantine city-state historically won Great Royal Wife rank for a daughter. Only great kings such as the Hittites did.
- **Ugarit.** Its fall is kept at its historical date, ~1192–1190.

## Proposed open questions
- Did her claim in Tyre come from shared rule with her brother or from being their father's favourite?
- Does her brother still rule Tyre in 1160, or has the claim passed to his children?
- Is her absence from Pi-Ramesses during the coup her own choice, or arranged by Tiye's side?
- Does Isis suspect the conspiracy before it strikes?
- Was she betrothed before or after the north fell? Was the marriage Tyre's insurance against Ugarit's fate?
- Did she arrange or press for Helen's marriage to Aegon?
- How does she regard Jon's choice to train with the Sherden?
- Did she take an Egyptian court name, or keep her Tyrian one?

## Hooks
- Her brother receives her in Tyre as the sister he once exported. Is she a war leader, a claimant, or a spent investment to be managed?
- The Egyptian archives still hold Tyre's vassal letters begging for water and wood. The new regime reads them aloud at court, or sends copies to Tyre.
- The Ugarit lesson comes true. Tyre calls on Egypt, Egypt is now Assyria's ally, and no relief comes.
- Titles become a battlefield. She refuses to name Pentawer as king or the Assyrian bride as queen, and every exchange becomes a negotiation over forms of address.
- Her Tyrian scribes and envoys, who write Akkadian, become the means of reaching Babylon or Elam after the coup, reviving Ramesses III's alliance without him.
- A reckoning with Aegon. She is forced to admit aloud what she always knew about his purple use. Later, if he redeems himself, she has to decide whether to claim the credit.
- Tiye, at the height of her power, names the thing Isis never says: that they were both upstarts, both married off for their families.
