This lore run sets Isis's backstory. She was born about 1205 into Tyre's royal house, which is only about two generations old; she knows Tyre's rulers were once Pharaoh's humble vassals, and her insistence on rank comes from that fragility. At 13 to 15 she watched from the island as Ugarit and the northern ports fell (about 1192 to 1190), and she took from it that allies arrive late and a city on the sea stands alone. She had a real claim to rule in Tyre, and her brother or the merchant houses behind him removed her by marrying her to Ramesses III (about 1185 to 1182) against her will. Tyre made her rank as Great Royal Wife a condition, paid a dowry of purple cloth, silver, cedar and ships, and sent her with a sworn Tyrian household that keeps its own budget and envoys inside the khener. She truly loves and respects Ramesses III (respect, not romance), and the two are real partners in the Assyrian war, which she wants to protect her home rather than for its own sake. Her contempt for Tiye is about rank, not jealousy. Aegon is the real fault line in her marriage: she knows roughly how bad his purple use is and shields him anyway. The author is emphatic that his death is a real grief to her, not only a political loss.
