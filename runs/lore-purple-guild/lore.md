# The Purple Guild

The purple guild is a priestly college based on Cyprus (Alashiya). It holds the only working method for turning the raw secretion of the murex dye-snail into the draw, the substance that gives nodes their power. Its finished members, the **nodes**, link mind to mind across any distance and relay the messages of kings word for word. That makes the guild the eastern Mediterranean's fastest channel of information, in a world where words travel fast and people travel slowly.

The guild's power was discovered on Minoan Crete, not in Tyre. Today, though, it depends on Tyre for its raw material. It is sworn to serve every paying crown and to belong to none.

Its name is not yet chosen. It will be a title derived from Minoan.

## Discovery on Minoan Crete

- **First use.** Purple's power was first noticed on Minoan Crete (Keftiu, to the Egyptians) around 1800 BCE. There, purple was a temple sacrament long before it was a trade good.
  - The palace priestesses dyed with murex.
  - In sanctuary rites they drank low doses of the gland liquor before it had ripened, as a visionary sacrament.
- **Visions came first; the shared voice came later.** The ability to communicate across distance appeared generations afterward, among the oldest priestesses, who had drunk the liquor all their lives.
  - The higher power was a discovery of old age and long practice, not of youth.
  - That pattern still shapes the guild: nodes are always adults.
- **Palace control.** The palace took control of the material, and the wanax claimed it.
  - In this world, the "royal purple" of the Knossos tablet KN X 976 is the drinking grade, not the cloth.
  - The rite became a secret discipline of the palace cult. It existed at Knossos alongside the hereditary griffin-keepers already in canon.
- **Not a women's order.** The first discoverers were priestesses, but the discipline was never exclusively female. The guild today is not mostly women either.
- **The secret stayed on Crete.** The dye trade spread east through Ugarit and Cyprus to the Canaanite coast. The sacrament and the training did not travel with it.

## The Tyrian story: Melqart's dog

- **What the myth is.** Tyre has a civic myth about the discovery of purple. Its god Melqart, whom the Greeks identify with Heracles, was walking the shore with his dog and the nymph Tyros, who personifies the city. The dog bit a sea snail and came up with a purple-stained muzzle. Tyros asked for a robe of that colour, and so the dye was found.
  - The myth gives a city's industry a divine pedigree and makes Tyre the discoverer.
  - In reality it is attested only much later, in Roman-era writers.
- **How the guild treats it.** In this world the myth is Tyre's public account. The guild treats it as a merchant city's polite fiction, covering an older truth.
- **Why it rankles in Tyre.** Tyre can make the dye but cannot make nodes. This gives Isis's house a standing reason to resent the guild.

## The fall of Knossos and the move to Alashiya

- **Escape by sea.** When Knossos fell, one to two centuries before the story, the purple priesthood survived. Its people could leave by sea, carrying the method in their heads.
  - The griffin-keepers did not survive. Their knowledge was never written down, and their animals went feral on Ida.
- **Why Cyprus.** The college moved east and settled on Cyprus. Cyprus already had its own murex works and copper wealth, and it stands between the great powers.
- **Its cult today.**
  - It is a religious college with its own cult, rooted in the Minoan goddess.
  - It is bound to no single kingdom's priesthood, Egyptian or otherwise.
- **No tie to Knossos.** The guild no longer reveres Knossos. It keeps no pilgrimage rights and no caretakers in the ruins where the Sherden train.

## The harvest

### The beds and the catch (Tyre)

- **Catching the snails.** Murex are taken from the beds off Tyre's coast.
  - Divers and trap-setters work the beds, setting baited wicker traps on floats over the deep water.
  - The snails are kept alive in seawater pools until they are used.
- **Making the dye.** In Tyre's dye yards, glands are opened in daylight, salted and boiled down in tin vessels. The secretion is nearly colourless and turns purple as light and air reach it.
  - This is the dye Tyre sells.
  - Canon's "few shades" follow the different snail species and treatments, from red-purple to blue-violet.
- **Scale.** The yields are tiny. It takes enormous numbers of snails to produce a little dye. The draw is just as scarce, which is part of why nodes are so few.

### The draw (the guild)

- **What the draw is.** The potency lies in the gland secretion *before* light touches it.
  - The guild's purple is not purple at all. It is the nearly colourless draw, unsunned.
  - Sunlight does not simply destroy the potency. It **changes** it.
- **Who processes it.** All processing of the draw is done by the guild, and none of it on the mainland. The guild obtains its raw material from Tyre through trade.
  - The accepted proposals originally placed a Tyrian royal dark-workshop on the island and split the catch on Tyre's shore. The author's note overrides both.
- **How it is processed.** The guild works in lamplit, windowless vault-workshops.
  - It seals the liquor in closed jars.
  - It stores the draw dried to powder in the dark.
- **Grades.** The strength of a dose for drinking is graded by how little light the draw has seen.
- **Weapon-working.** Purple worked into weapons and armour uses the raw precursor, not the finished dye. This overrides the earlier wording that weapons used sunned purple.
- **The recreational grade.** Sunned dregs, nearly spent, are the low-dose psychedelic sold for pleasure.

### Tyre's hold on the supply

- **Tyre controls the supply.** Its rise to dominance over northern Canaan rests on its access to the material.
- **A lever both ways.**
  - Tyre's royal house knows the supply is its strongest lever over the guild. Isis grew up knowing it, and it feeds her sense of entitlement.
  - The dependence is mutual. The guild needs Tyre's beds; Tyre cannot make what the guild makes.
- **Assyria.** Assyria's raids on northern Canaan are partly aimed at the dye coast.

## Recreational purple

- **The trade.** The pleasure trade in sunned dregs is a black market.
  - The cheap stuff is weak and the strong stuff is rare.
  - A bad batch is dangerous.
- **Aegon.** Aegon's party purple is these dregs.
  - He is high on them the night someone dies protecting him. That death is not necessarily an overdose.
  - Years of abuse leave him scarred.
- **The guild's view.** To the guild, a prince playing with scraps of its sacrament is a profanation. The guild keeps account of who has profaned it.

## Becoming a node

### Entry

- Candidates enter in youth. They are sons and daughters of good families.
- Great families compete for places, as they do for the House of Life. A family with a node among its children gains standing at every court.
- The guild's schools are attached to its temples and run like the House of Life. They train scribes, diplomats and priests.

### The ladder

Formation takes ten to fourteen years, modelled on Jesuit formation. A novice entering at 16–18 becomes a node at about 28–32. There are formal examinations between stages. The rank names below are placeholders; other accepted material calls the lower ranks Candidates and Adepts.

| Stage | Length | What it involves |
|---|---|---|
| **Novice** | ~2 years | Oaths and low ceremonial doses. Scribal and language training: hieratic, Akkadian (the diplomatic language), the guild's own script and formulae, and the protocols of correspondence between great kings. |
| **Listener** | ~3–4 years | Steadily rising doses. Hears faintly, at first only in sleep, and senses other dosed minds at close range. Learns to tell signal from vision. Trains in pairs. |
| **Regency** | ~2 years | Posted to a provincial temple or port under supervision. |
| **Voice** | ~3 years | Protocol, and memorizing the forms of "the Compact" (a placeholder name). |
| **Node** | for life | Final dose, final oaths, court posting. |

The accepted material describes Regency and Voice candidates doing relay and distance work. It also rules that only nodes can link. How the lower ranks practise is not yet settled.

### The final dose

- **The ordeal.** The ladder ends in a final, dangerous dose.
  - Some adepts do not survive it. Others come out unable to link.
  - The guild's prestige therefore carries a quiet death toll.
- **What survivors gain.** Survivors gain no ancestral memory. They gain a lifelong tether to the other nodes, which they can never fully switch off.

### Those who fail

- Candidates who fail do not lose their rank. They become guild archivists and envoys, temple scribes or provincial voices.
- This is a respectable career, and even failure is prestigious outside the guild. Inside guild houses it is felt as a quiet disappointment.

## What a node is

- **Who they are.** Nodes are mature, highly educated adults: diplomats and archivists as much as relays.
- **Celibacy and name.** They are celibate, and they give up their family name at the final dose.
- **Bound for life.** Nodes cannot leave the guild; the high dose binds them.
- **How the link works.** A node can reach every other node, and only nodes.
  - In function they are canon's "payphones": fixed points through which a court can speak to another court.
- **Fidelity.** Relay is word for word.
  - Fidelity is the node's pride.
  - A node who alters a message disgraces the whole college.
- **The cost.** The danger of the long ingestion is erasure, not madness: the sense of self thins. Nodes are always "hearing the line" and are permanently a little elsewhere.
  - A node may keep a private object, such as a seal or a song, to remind themselves which thoughts are their own.
- **Standing.**
  - Nodes wear purple-striped robes, like Aegean cult figures. In a world where purple means royalty, they are among the few non-royals entitled to it.
  - They are housed in temples or palaces.
  - Senior nodes sit at court beside the high priests.

## The oath

- **The vow.** The guild swears to carry every message for every paying crown and never to reveal one.
- **The penalty.** A node who reveals a message is put to death by the guild itself.
- **It is truly kept.** The guild does not sell even the record of who wrote to whom.
- **Why courts trust it.** Courts accept the guild's law, and that acceptance is the basis of its prestige. Kings trust it because it has no king.
- **Inviolability.** Ordinary envoys can be detained for years or mistreated. Guild nodes are never touched, because every court needs them and none can afford to be cut off.
- **The price.** Kings pay heavily to keep a node at court.
- **Who it serves.** The guild serves the pharaoh, whoever holds the throne.

## The guild and the conspiracy

- **The traffic.** The conspirators use the guild. In the same season, the guild carries their traffic and Ramesses III's own messages, and it says nothing.
- **The Senior Node.**
  - She relays coded conspiracy traffic without decoding it.
  - Later she half-recognizes a phrase from Tiye's household and tests her own oath by saying nothing.
  - After the coup she must look at what she carried. That makes her a possible witness, whom Tiye may need silenced or won over.
- **Timing the killing.** Tiye and the nobles must either time the killing around the Pi-Ramesses node's duties, or count on the guild's neutrality to report events without taking sides.
- **Spreading the news.**
  - When Ramesses III dies, the Pi-Ramesses node relays the news.
  - Isis learns of her husband's death through the guild, and so does the Assyrian court.
- **The guild's answer.** "We carried; we do not judge." It is both honourable and damning.
- **After the coup.**
  - The guild serves Pentawer.
  - Its supply now runs through a Tyre at war with Egypt's new ally, Assyria.
  - It faces a choice: lean toward Tyre, or try to grow beds elsewhere, such as Cyprus or Crete's old Minoan dye coves.

## Established figures

- **The Senior Node** is a POV character.
  - Her discipline is her identity, and her fear is erasure.
  - When the Littlefinger figure asks her what *she* thinks, it unsettles her more than any threat could.
- **The Pi-Ramesses node** is a highborn adult who spent their youth on the ladder: respected, wealthy, and permanently a little elsewhere.
- **Aegon** comes to the guild after the coup and asks for help.
  - He is refused at first and has to earn a hearing.
  - Meeting a node shows him the substance he played with turned into discipline instead of escape.
  - Getting sober is his first deliberate act. Isis's fury over the early death focuses on the purple.

## Contradictions at the guild's core

- **No king, one city.** It is sworn to no king, yet its life depends on one city's beds and on the good will of that city's royal house.
- **Rooted in a cult it left behind.** Its cult is rooted in the Minoan goddess, yet it has abandoned Knossos, the place where that cult was born.
- **Fidelity as complicity.** Its honour is fidelity, and fidelity is exactly what lets it carry the traffic that kills a king.
- **Prestige bought with a death toll.** Its prestige is built on candidates who die or come out broken at the final dose.
- **Neutral but obliged.** It is neutral by oath, yet it serves whoever sits on Egypt's throne.

## Departures from the historical record

- **Invented magic.** Murex purple was only a dye. Its psychoactive and telepathic properties, and the potency of the unsunned secretion, belong to this world alone. The photochemistry is real; the magical rule built on it is not.
- **The KN X 976 tablet.** Reading "royal purple" as a drinking grade is invented.
- **Minoan priority is real**, as far as current scholarship goes.
- **Messenger immunity is reversed.** Real Late Bronze Age messengers had no secure immunity. The guild's inviolability reverses that pattern.
- **Tyre's dominance is early.** Tyre's dominance of purple in this period is earlier than the archaeology supports. Large-scale Levantine production is best attested from the 10th century BCE on.
- **The Melqart myth is late.** Historically it is a late (Roman-era) attestation. Here it is an established civic myth by 1160.

## Hooks

- **The founding legend.** Two long-dosed Minoan priestesses first hear each other across the sea, between Knossos and the Chrysi dye-beds.
- **A forgotten shrine.** The guild has abandoned Knossos, but an oldest shrine of the purple cult could lie sealed in the ruins for Jon to stumble on.
- **News reaches Jon first.** A node relays the coup to Crete long before any ship could, and Jon hears his father is dead from a stranger's mouth.
- **The Assyrian channel.** The guild may be the channel through which the Assyrian marriage was negotiated before the coup, carried word for word and never revealed.
- **"We carried; we do not judge."** Isis or Jon learns the guild carried the conspiracy. Isis pushes Tyre to squeeze the supply in punishment, even if that blinds her own children's allies.
- **Tiye's weapon.** Tiye sends her orders through the guild precisely because she knows its fidelity means it will not interfere.
- **The Littlefinger leak.** The Littlefinger figure cultivates the Senior Node, and her private object becomes the thing he learns to use.
- **Executing its own.** The guild executes one of its own for revealing a message, and the audience at court sees the oath's price.
- **Breaking the monopoly.** The guild secretly seeds beds on Cyprus or in Crete's old dye coves, a silkworm-theft move that could end Tyre's hold.
- **The cutoff clock.** An Assyrian raid on the dye coast puts a countdown on how long the guild's stored draw lasts.
- **A death at the final dose.** Someone tied to a main character dies at the final dose, revealing the toll behind the guild's prestige.
- **The prince at the door.** Aegon stands refused at the guild's door, scarred by years of the dregs, and has to earn the hearing a node once gave up everything to deserve.
- **The griffin's fear.** The thing that frightens Jon's griffin in its Bucephalus moment could be the smell of purple-worked bronze, the raw precursor in Sherden weapons.
