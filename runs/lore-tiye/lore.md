# Tiye

Tiye is a secondary wife of Pharaoh Ramesses III and the mother of his son Pentawer. She is common-born, of a Sherden soldier-farmer family, and was married to the king at about fifteen without being asked. In 1160 she is about thirty, has spent half her life in the khener at Pi-Ramesses, and is preparing the conspiracy that will kill the king and crown her son. In this world it succeeds.

## At a glance (1160 BCE)
- **Born:** around 1190. This is derived from her age, and puts her birth in the first year of the War of Succession.
- **Married:** around 1175, at about fifteen. She bore Pentawer almost at once.
- **Rank:** secondary wife of Ramesses III. Her rank was granted to her, not inherited.
- **Residence:** the khener at Pi-Ramesses.
- **Family:**
  - father Tywin, a Sherden plot-holder;
  - mother Olenna, once a handmaiden in the khener who rose to overseer of the women's household;
  - an unnamed brother who commands archers on the Nubian frontier;
  - her son Pentawer, about fifteen.
- **On the page at story open:** she reads as the woman she was at fifteen, innocent and naive. The political shrewdness and cunning she has developed since have not yet been shown.

## Origins

### The family's class
Tiye's father's family are hereditary Sherden. They hold heritable plots of about 3 to 5 arouras in Middle Egypt, near the harem-town of Mer-wer at the mouth of the Fayum. Before the match they were well-off farmers in service, not nobles.
- Service in the corps passes from father to son.
- The original Sherden came from Sardinia, so Tiye's "common birth" is soldier-class and of foreign descent, not peasant.

### Two sources of the rise
The family's rise has two sources, and Tiye knows which one was decisive.

1. **The deed.** Tywin, then a common soldier, saved Ramesses III's life, about two years before Tiye's marriage, when she was about thirteen. He received the standard rewards for valour before the king:
   - gold of valour, given in public;
   - captives;
   - extra fields.

   The deed also won the family an audience, and that was all it won.
2. **The door.** Olenna, a servant in the royal khener, turned the audience into a bed. She placed her thirteen-year-old daughter where the king would see her again. Over the next two years she worked her into a marriage.

The court tells the soldier's story, because it flatters the king. The truth is the handmaid's, and Tiye knows it.

## Formative event: the match
Tiye was not asked. She learned of her marriage as a done deal, arranged by her mother on the strength of her father's deed.

When she married, Isis had been Great Royal Wife for the better part of a decade, and Aegon was a small child. Tiye's son was therefore born with no road to the throne.

The family's present wealth is the price that was paid for her at fifteen, and Tiye knows that too. Her cunning was learned partly from watching how she herself was spent.

## Family

### Tywin (father)
- He was a Sherden plot-holder from Middle Egypt.
- His rescue of the king is the family's public story and the source of its new gold and land.
- The family's manners are still those of Sherden farmers, which is exactly what the old families sneer at.
- Whether he is alive in 1160 is open. If he is, he is visibly rich, and he may be one of Ramesses III's top generals, possibly a Sherden commander. That is undecided.

### Olenna (mother)
- She served in the royal khener. For a time she was a servant in the Great Royal Wife's own apartments, so Tiye is the daughter of Isis's own servant.
- She climbed the khener's staff from handmaiden to an overseer of the women's household. Tiye came to the king's notice through that office.
- She is the true author of the match.
- Whether she is alive in 1160 is open. If she is, she is visibly rich.

### The surviving parent
Whichever parent survives rose on Tiye's marriage.
- **Tiye's side:** she loves that parent and relies on them, and she quietly cannot forgive them. Her lie is "my family is the only ally I have."
- **The parent's side:** their lie is "we gave her a better life."
- **The result:** each uses the other. This makes the parent the conspiracy's natural first recruit and also its likeliest betrayer.

### The brother
- He commands archers on the Nubian frontier.
- In the conspiracy he is the outside rising. At the agreed signal, a letter from his sister inside the khener tells him to stir up the garrison.
- The trouble in the south draws attention and loyal troops away from Pi-Ramesses at the moment of the killing.
- His command ties Tiye's family to the Nubian nobility that controls the gold mines. The exact relationship is undefined.
- His name, his age relative to Tiye, and how he came to his command are not established.

### Pentawer (son)
- He is about fifteen in 1160, roughly the age Tiye was when she was given away.
- After the coup he becomes pharaoh and marries a princess of Assyria. Once again a match is made for policy.
- Tiye deliberately keeps him ignorant of the plot, for two reasons:
  - so that no one can ever call him a parricide;
  - because she wants him to stay the innocent she was at fifteen.
- She calls this protection. In fact she is raising a king who does not know what his throne cost.

## Position in the khener
Tiye has lived in the khener at Pi-Ramesses since she was about fifteen. It is her mother's old world as well as her own.

The khener there also holds Isis's sworn Tyrian household, a small Tyre with its own budget and envoys. Tiye lives in close proximity to the woman who despises her.

## Resources: her mother's people
Tiye inherits Olenna's network inside the khener whether or not Olenna is alive:
- the pantry chief;
- the clerk;
- the gate-wives, the wives of the men at the harem gate who live inside.

These are the people the court looks past, and the conspiracy runs on them. Outside the walls, her reach runs through her brother's frontier command and through whatever rank her surviving parent holds.

## Loyalties and debts
- **To Pentawer:** a future. She can give him one only by killing his father.
- **To her surviving parent:** love, dependence, and an unforgiven debt.
- **To her brother:** she will spend him as she was spent, by asking him to raise a revolt with a letter.
- **To the nobles:** a deal. They want Ramesses III gone to save the cost of the Assyrian war, and she wants her son crowned.
- **To the older princes:** she needs them away, not dead. No royal children die in the coup, and Isis survives.
- **To Ramesses III:** not established. See the open questions.

## Inner contradictions
- **She was spent, and now she spends.** She resents being traded at fifteen, yet her plan depends on her brother, her mother's people and her son's marriage being used the same way.
- **She protects her son by keeping him blind.** She shields Pentawer from the crime as Isis shields Aegon from his unfitness. Both mothers call it love.
- **Her family is her ally and her wound.** She cannot forgive the parent who arranged her life, and she cannot act without them.
- **She mirrors Isis.** Isis's private fear is that one slip would make her "a servant's daughter again" in the old court's eyes. Tiye actually is a servant's daughter, and the servant was Isis's own. In the old court's eyes both women are upstarts, and neither admits it.

## Wants and fears
- **Wants:** Pentawer on the throne, and never again to be looked down on.
- **Grievance:** her resentment of being looked down on grows every year.
- **Fears:** nothing further is established.

## How others see her
- **Isis** holds that secondary wives are beneath jealousy. Her contempt for Tiye is about rank and birth, not the bed, and it is personal, because Tiye's mother served in Isis's apartments. Isis's weapon is form: she will not address Tiye by a rank she has not granted. Tiye must nonetheless put Isis on a pedestal, as everyone must.
- **The court** knows Tiye as the soldier's daughter, the reward for a deed. Few know the match was her mother's work.
- **The old families** sneer at the Sherden-farmer manners of her newly rich kin.
- **The nobles** treat her as a partner in removing the king.

## Departures from the historical record
- **The plot succeeds.** Historically the conspiracy failed, and Tiye's punishment is unrecorded. Pentawer keeps his real name, but here he is crowned rather than tried, and he is kept ignorant of the plot.
- **Her family is invented.** The historical record gives Tiye no father, mother, birthplace or titles. Tywin, Olenna and the brother contradict no evidence. The brother is modelled on Binemwese, the archer captain who was condemned over a letter from his sister in the harem.
- **The Sherden landholding is a mild anachronism.** Hereditary Sherden plots in Middle Egypt are documented in the Wilbour Papyrus, which dates from the reign of Ramesses V, a little after 1160.
- **A name clash to be aware of.** In the real court, Ramesses III's mother was named Tiy-Merenese. Canon does not address her.

## Canon conflicts
- **Who chose Pentawer's bride.** The accepted proposal "Tiye keeps Pentawer clean" says Tiye chooses his foreign bride for policy. The same proposal also leaves open whether Tiye or the nobles arranged the Assyrian marriage, and q-6d73e460 remains open. This document leaves it open. The options are:
  - answer q-6d73e460 as Tiye, making the mirror of her own marriage exact;
  - assign the match to the nobles, so that Pentawer's marriage is done over her head as hers was;
  - make it joint.

## Proposed open questions
- Which of Tywin and Olenna is alive in 1160, and what rank does the survivor hold?
- At which battle did Tywin save the king? The Year 8 Delta battle (~1177) fits; a Year 11 Libyan war (~1174) would fall after the marriage.
- Did Tywin know Olenna was working the match, or does he believe his deed earned it?
- Does Isis know, or remember, that Tiye is the daughter of her own former servant?
- What did Tiye feel for Ramesses III when she married him, and what does she feel now?
- Once Pentawer is crowned, does the brother stay loyal, or set a price of his own?
- Does the brother's southern revolt draw one of the older princes away from Pi-Ramesses?
- Does Pentawer truly not know about the plot, or does he choose not to ask?

## Hooks
- Isis's refusal to grant Tiye a rank becomes unbearable once both know the secret: the servant's daughter is about to displace her mother's mistress.
- Someone who learns the handmaid's version of the match, perhaps the Littlefinger figure, uses it to shame Pentawer's reign: "a throne bought by a serving-woman's scheming."
- If Tywin commands the Sherden, Jon's training on Crete is in the hands of Pentawer's grandfather. That would be Tiye's quiet lever for keeping the youngest prince away.
- The signal letter to the brother is the one piece of the plot that might travel by hand rather than through the guild, whose oath never lets a record leak. A surviving letter would be evidence.
- The surviving parent, the likeliest betrayer, sells Tiye out after the coup to protect the fortune her marriage bought.
- After the coup the old families strike at Tiye's kin first, as they did at the Woodvilles, so the fate of her family measures her power.
- The Assyrian princess arrives at about fifteen, unasked, and Tiye has to decide whether to treat her as Isis treated Tiye.
- Pentawer learns what his throne cost, and from whom, and turns his mother's "protection" back on her.
